23 July 2026

Dorset Council Local Plan delayed: strategic update

Dorset Council has officially announced that it will not meet the statutory deadline to progress the Dorset Local Plan under the previous planning framework.

  • Transition to New NPPFThe council is transitioning to the updated National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) system, which is anticipated to extend the Local Plan adoption timeline by up to 30 months.
  • Impact on the Vale of AllenWith no specific site allocations currently earmarked for our parishes in the draft Dorset Local Plan, maintaining momentum on our Neighbourhood Plan is essential.
  • Next stepsThe Steering Group Chair and committee are taking expert planning advice to ensure our emerging policies—covering heritage, environment, housing, and local business, with youth needs considered throughout—provide immediate statutory protection against speculative or unsympathetic development across our villages.
5 July 2026

Working groups draft policy frameworks

Following the survey analysis, five dedicated working groups are now translating community feedback and evidence into statutory planning policies. The Youth Working Group works across every theme so young people's needs shape the whole plan:

  1. 01

    Environment

    Guidelines on protecting local green spaces, walking routes, dark skies, and biodiversity.

  2. 02

    Heritage

    Mapping designated and non designated heritage assets across all villages, such as historic churches, cob walls, and ancient roadways, starting with initial audits in Hinton Martell and Witchampton.

  3. 03

    Housing

    Establishing local connection criteria, housing register alignment, and criteria based policies for infill housing.

  4. 04

    Business & Economy

    Gathering evidence to support rural enterprises, agriculture, and home working infrastructure.

  5. 05

    Youth

    Bringing young people's needs and aspirations into the evidence, priorities, and recommendations developed by every working group.

16 June 2026

Community survey completed: 269 responses received across the parishes

Following the recent leaflet drop to every household across the Vale of Allen, the Neighbourhood Plan Steering Group has compiled and analyzed the full results of the community questionnaire.

Key survey findings & statistics

  • High community engagement: 269 questionnaires were returned out of 655 households, representing a 41% household response rate across the parish area.
  • Population represented: The responses capture the views of 541 residents aged 16+ (40.8% of the local adult population).

  • Valued local assets: Residents rated a friendly and safe environment, open green spaces, proximity to woodland and rivers, and inclusion within the Cranborne Chase National Landscape and Dark Skies Reserve as the most vital aspects of living in the Vale.
  • Housing development pace: 89% of respondents favoured very modest growth (less than 2 new homes per year), with an overwhelming preference for brownfield development and individual infill plots over greenfield sites.
  • Local economy & remote working: 36.6% of respondents work from home, 14.9% run a business from home, and 46% support reserving land for small scale local business development.
  • Infrastructure concerns: The top infrastructure priorities identified by residents include road conditions (66.9%), bus services (43.8%), car parking (40.2%), surface water drainage (38%), and broadband speed (36.5%).

24 February 2026Community Consultation

Launch of village specific residents' questionnaires

The Steering Group launched tailored village questionnaires for all constituent settlements across the parish, including Crichel, Gussage All Saints, Gussage St Michael, Hinton, and Witchampton.

  • To capture local community needs while building a single unified Vale of Allen Neighbourhood Plan, surveys were customised to each village's distinct infrastructure, assets, and features.
  • Residents provided input on local character, infrastructure priorities, housing views, business support, and environmental conservation through the end of February 2026.
21 July 2025Plan Governance & Boundary Setup

Boundary designation progress and Steering Committee formation

Following the initial village launch meetings, the Parish Council confirmed completion of the three month public boundary consultation with Dorset Council, which ran until 17 June 2025.

  • Dorset Council received five representations: four from statutory agencies offering guidance and one from an adjacent parish suggesting boundary refinements.
  • On 8 July 2025, the Parish Council formally agreed to proceed with the proposed boundary covering the full Vale of Allen Parish area.
  • Volunteer Steering Committee members representing each village were recruited to lead the next phases of plan development.
6 May 2025Statutory Submissions

Application to Dorset Council for Neighbourhood Area designation

Vale of Allen Parish Council formally submitted its Neighbourhood Area application to Dorset Council.

  • The designation application included formal agreed consent from neighbouring Knowlton Parish Council, where the proposed boundary extends slightly into the adjacent parish area.
  • Dorset Council published the application for a statutory consultation period through 17 June 2025.
11 March 2025Project Inception

Parish Council resolution to proceed

Following a series of community launch meetings held across each village in the Vale, the Vale of Allen Parish Council formally voted to initiate a statutory Neighbourhood Plan.

Key detail

Community feedback during the launch meetings demonstrated strong local consensus to create a community led planning document guiding future development, landscape preservation, and rural sustainability.
