About the plan

One rural landscape,
nine distinct communities.

The plan area follows the shared geography of the Vale of Allen, bringing neighbouring settlements together around a practical vision for future development.

The planning framework

Local priorities with statutory weight

Neighbourhood planning allows communities to prepare planning policies for their area. When brought into force, the Vale of Allen Neighbourhood Plan will form part of the statutory development plan used by Dorset Council when deciding planning applications.

The plan must meet national requirements and work alongside Dorset Council's strategic planning policies. It will use proportionate local evidence to address matters such as the scale and location of development, design, landscape, heritage and community needs.

How the plan comes into force

Once a complete draft has been prepared, Vale of Allen Parish Council must publicise it for at least six weeks and consult the prescribed consultation bodies. Consultation responses will be considered and the plan amended where appropriate before it is submitted to Dorset Council.

Dorset Council will check that the submitted plan complies with the relevant legal requirements, publicise it for a further minimum six weeks and appoint an independent examiner. The examiner will consider whether the plan meets the basic conditions and issue a report. Dorset Council must then decide whether the plan should proceed to referendum.

If more than half of those voting in the referendum vote in favour, the plan comes into force as part of the statutory development plan. Subject to the narrow exceptions set out in legislation, Dorset Council must then formally make the neighbourhood plan within eight weeks of the referendum.

The area lies within and around the setting of the Cranborne Chase National Landscape. Its nationally important landscape character, dark skies, biodiversity and historic environment are central considerations, guided by the National Landscape's planning guidance as well as local and national policy.
